# siteplan-engine: Agent Instructions

> Last verified: 2026-10-19

An offline "survey to site plan" assistant for a Hyderabad architecture firm (agency client). It reads a surveyor's drawing, checks a proposal against Telangana building rules, prints the firm's area statement, and writes DXF using BuildNow plugin layer names. It is client-facing software: tests, lint and review apply.

## Architecture

The permanent pipeline and its plan are in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`: raw survey → CanonicalSiteModel → ResolvedRules → BuildableEnvelope → TowerPrototype → optimizer → CandidateLayout → independent ValidationReport → (later) LLM strategy → CAD/report, with DesignBrief carrying the architect's program and PartitionLedger / RuleLayers the two kinds of area accounting. The contracts are code in `src/siteplan/contracts/` (authoritative); `docs/contracts/*.schema.json` is generated from them. `src/siteplan/adapters/` converts the prototype's Project, LayoutRequest and LayoutOption to the contracts and back without changing a number, so the prototype keeps working while stages move over. Prototype version tagged `v0.9-prototype` (1af547a). Wave 1 stages, on contracts 1.1: `legal/` (ResolvedRules and the BuildableEnvelope), `prototypes/` (the tower prototype kit), `optimizer/` (floors from metres, the guard, Pareto alternatives, today's generator as the LEGACY strategy) and `validator/` (the independent ValidationReport, the optimizer's guard; it imports no generator module). Wave 2, on contracts 1.2: Table III below 21 m in `legal/` (A2), the validator's checks for blocks below 21 m (D2), and the full search in `optimizer/search/` (C2: strategy FULL beside LEGACY, the blocks placed first and the circulation drawn from them). C3: a floor count below 21 m is offered on its own band's permission (`optimizer/floors.py`: ALLOWED, or UNVERIFIED and labelled so; never PROHIBITED, never a band with no setback), and the full search places such blocks wherever their band's land allows, in the columns and on the ground the ring road leaves (`optimizer/search/fringe.py`), each served by a road or, up to 12 m, a rule 8(l) pathway; it searches every profile with them and without, and says for each narrow part of the plot whether one fits there and whether the layouts proposed use it (since 2026-10-19 also the narrow parts of the land each kind of low block keeps to by its band's setbacks, so an arm wider than the net plot's 30 m split is tried: Dhulapally's north arm, which the note had judged by its 525 m² tip alone). LEGACY still lays out high-rise counts only. C4-01: the full search draws every road from a centre-line graph (`optimizer/search/road_graph.py`: entrances, junctions and the blocks pathways serve as nodes, each road's centre line as edges, the pavement the line widened), and refuses a layout whose roads are not one network or stop with nowhere to go; the validator measures the pavement as before. C4-02: every configuration is also tried with further clusters on the ground the first leaves, each round its own ring road and joined by a link road (or where the rings meet), up to three (`Config.more_clusters`). C4-03: a further cluster is also tried in its own ground's directions (`layout.zone_angles`), its blocks carrying their own frame (`Standing.frame`). C4-04: where the kit has blocks of more than one depth, every configuration is also tried with columns of each depth (`Config.mixed_depths`), laid out after all of the others with quotas of their own. C4-05: the fringe tries every kind of block in turn (a smaller or lower one where a larger has no room) and is laid in the plot's own directions as well as the configuration's (`fringe.FRINGE_DIRECTIONS`), the most valuable kept. C4-06: a floor count is dropped only behind a taller one alike in everything it asks and rests on (`readings.resources`), and the profile built for every reading takes no block below 21 m that NBC's own 15 m line holds to 4.6's fire access, so its layouts hold under both lines. C4-09: each profile's judged layouts are its front and different ideas before copies (`strategy._to_judge`), and a fourth alternative, ROBUST (`ParetoPoint.ROBUST`), shows the layout that rests on the fewest open readings, the most saleable between equals. C4-10: the facilities are laid in the order (the firm's, its rotations, the largest first, within the classes) that places the most. C4-12: the open space is taken where it is usable, at least 12 m across and within 30 m of a block (`objective.usable_open_sqm`), the most usable pockets first, and the open-space axis scores that part. C4-13: a cellar is dug only as far as the need takes it: once the fewest levels are known, a region square to the turn grown round its ramp, the piece of the plot under the setback that the ramp reaches and that holds the need by floor area and by cars laid out, the same on every level (`parking_plan._sized`); the cellar setback a candidate reports is the rule's band. ## Rules

- **Client data never enters git.** Drawings and anything derived from them live in `fixtures/` and `out/`, both gitignored. Examples and tests use made-up geometry.
- **No attribution trailers.** Commits and PRs carry no `Co-Authored-By`, `Claude-Session` or "Generated with" lines, whatever a session's defaults say.
- **Site facts, law and design intent are kept apart** (`src/siteplan/contracts/`): CanonicalSiteModel holds what the land is and how each fact is known; ResolvedRules what the law asks, heights in metres and never a floor count, every open reading an Interpretation (selected ALL means every consumer evaluates every reading, and a result that holds under only some is UNVERIFIED; since contracts 1.4, 2026-10-13, the validator evaluates every reading of a question the law leaves open whatever a study selected, only a settled one, VERIFIED or not a question of law, alone: a result that differs is CONDITIONAL, keeps each reading's result in `by_reading`, names what it rests on in `rests_on` (an open reading, a site fact, a gap in the rules or a measurement) and gives the result under the study's selection in `selected_status`, and the report's `verdict.study` beside its verdict; the optimizer's guard and the export refuse on the study's verdict, so generation keeps to the architect's selections, and a report stored as 1.3 reads its UNVERIFIED as CONDITIONAL and keeps its digest); DesignBrief what the architect and the firm want. `tests/test_contracts.py` enforces it. Net ownership is gross ownership less only land leaving the title (surrender, acquisition, transfer): a setback, buffer, fire band or green strip never reduces it. The PartitionLedger counts every square metre of the net site once by its physical use; RuleLayers carry the regulatory geometry, overlap, and are never summed.

A dot or dash beside a cross-section counts as crossed: Suchitra's footpaths are dotted, and without them its 12.4 m (40 ft) road read as a 9 m carriageway. Widths are as drawn and may be the carriageway alone (Dhulapally's survey draws 7-14 m where its site plan draws a 60 ft entry road), so the floors calculator and the `max_floors` tool take the legal width from the architect and never from the drawing.
- **A DWG is read in its own world coordinates and its own declared unit** (`dxf_entities.py`, used by the survey reader and the flat importer). The header's $INSUNITS says what one unit is (Dhulapally's site plan: inches; Suchitra's floor plans: millimetres), and a file that does not say is refused rather than guessed: the flat importer once assumed millimetres, which on an inch drawing puts rooms 25.4 times too far apart. Every block is opened out by its own placement (position, scale, rotation, nested blocks too), and its layer-0 contents take the block's layer, as CAD shows them; before this a boundary or a level drawn inside a block was not read at all. Positions are taken in world terms because a mirrored block turns its contents' own coordinate system over. A DWG carries no map location unless a surveyor georeferences it; the firm's does not.
- **The printed scale on a survey sheet is not trusted.** Scale comes from the dimension labels, then snaps to a standard plot scale only when within 0.5% (Dhulapally's was plotted to fit the paper at 1:744.9; all seven labels agree to 0.01%). Every closed shape of every colour is tried as the plot except the sheet frame: keeping the largest shape per colour once returned Suchitra's black frame, 7,239 m², for its black plot of 8,063.8 m². Levels are the sheet's own densest run of decimals, because one survey levels from sea level (583 m) and another from a benchmark of 100.000. A sheet with no dimension labels (Suchitra) is scaled from its written area, the plot being the tightest shape round the spot levels, with a warning that the area is then not an independent check. Areas are read in acres and guntas (with trailing square yards), square metres and square yards, never square feet, and the site area is the largest written.
- The BuildNow layer names in `dxf_export.py` are from the plugin's layer index; whether the plugin accepts pre-named layers is untested (needs Windows, ZWCAD 2025 and the plugin).
