# Production architecture

> Last verified: 2026-10-03 (approved 2026-10-02, revision 2 with four semantic corrections;
> contracts 1.1 approved 2026-10-03 with three clarifications)

The prototype (tag `v0.9-prototype`, 1af547a) proved the rules on two real sites. This document
is the permanent architecture it moves to, stage by stage, without breaking what works. The
contracts are code (`src/siteplan/contracts/`); this file says what each is for and the rules
they keep. Field lists live in the code and in `docs/contracts/*.schema.json` (generated by
`uv run siteplan schema`), never here.

## 1. Pipeline

```
raw survey ──► CanonicalSiteModel ──► ResolvedRules ──► BuildableEnvelope
                    (site facts)        (law, metres)     (legal land, no towers, no roads)
                                                                  │
DesignBrief (architect's program, firm standards) ──┐             ▼
TowerPrototype library (real buildings) ────────────┼──► optimizer ──► CandidateLayout
                                                    │                        │
                                                    └──► independent validator ──► ValidationReport
                                                                             │
                                          LLM architectural strategy (tool API only, later)
                                                                             │
                                                                       CAD / report
```

## 2. The contracts

Separation (tests/test_contracts.py): **site facts, law and design intent never share a
contract.**

- **CanonicalSiteModel** (`site_model.py`): what the land is and how each fact is known
  (`Sourced`: value, status, source kind, source). It holds the ownership reconciliation, the
  net outline when it can be placed (None while a deduction's location is unknown: the pipeline
  stops and asks), the roads with the access road's legal width kept apart from the drawn one,
  water as drawn (its buffer is law), jurisdiction, coordinates.
- **DesignBrief** (`design_brief.py`): the architect's program (unit mix, units, club house,
  amenities and whether each belongs in the club house, parking preferences), the height intent
  ("Stilt + N" notation with its evidence, the most the law allows, fixed or a range; mixed
  heights allowed), the firm's standards (stilt and floor-to-floor heights, loading, cellar
  standards, longest block, cores), the design margins kept in hand above the legal minimums
  (none unless the firm sets them) and the optimizer's objectives (the three Pareto points,
  priorities, soft preferences). No site fact, no legal value.
- **ResolvedRules** (`resolved_rules.py`): what the law asks of this site. Every value carries
  clause, basis and status. **Heights are limits in metres on a named measure** (the Table IV
  height, the physical height NBC measures, AMSL); there is no floor count anywhere. Open
  questions are Interpretations with their readings; `selected: ALL` means every consumer
  evaluates every reading and a result that holds under only some is UNVERIFIED. Required:
  `stilt_in_rule_height` (ALL), `open_space_basis` (ALL), `circulation_in_setback` (ALL),
  `tot_lot_surface` (ALL), `open_space_other_uses` (ALL), `fire_turning_radius`,
  `approach_width`, `mixed_height_spacing`, `visitor_parking`, `amenity_share`.
- **BuildableEnvelope** (`envelope.py`): statutory exclusions fixed in place (water buffers, HT
  corridors), the setback envelope and buildable land per height band, the width profile
  (regions with their inscribed widths, **reported, never judged**: whether a narrow arm can take
  a small tower, a low block or a club house is the optimizer's question), circulation as
  **requirements** (access zones, gate, road and fire obligations, pointing at ResolvedRules),
  the quantities asked (pointers, the numbers live in ResolvedRules), rule layers and facts. It
  places no tower and **no road**.
- **TowerPrototype** (`prototype.py`): one typical floor of a reusable building in its own
  frame: footprint, flat modules, cores (lift/stair placeholders), corridor, per-floor numbers
  (saleable from the modules only). No legal value, no floor count; floor heights only when the
  design needs its own (else the brief's).
- **CandidateLayout** (`candidate.py`): one proposal: prototypes placed (position, rotation,
  mirror, floors each), roads and gates, open space, club house, amenities, ramps, cellars, bays,
  the generator's PartitionLedger and RuleLayers, metrics and the generator's own claims.
  Heights are not stored; whoever needs them derives them.
- **ValidationReport** (`validation.py`): an independent verdict from the site model, the rules,
  the brief and the candidate, recomputing what it checks; the envelope only for cross-checking.
  Legal verdict FAIL if any legal check fails or a discrepancy blocks a pass, else UNVERIFIED if
  any is unverified, else PASS; NOT_CHECKED listed beside it; the program verdict (mix, units,
  amenities asked) never changes the legal one. Design targets are listed beside the verdict:
  the legal minimum, the target the brief's margin puts above it, and what is provided.

### The four semantic corrections (2026-10-02)

1. **Roads inside setbacks are not settled law.** `circulation_in_setback` is an
   UNRESOLVED_INTERPRETATION selected ALL. In every SETBACK rule layer, ROAD and
   FIRE_HARDSTANDING carry a CONDITIONAL permission referring to it; never ALLOWED. The optimizer
   and the validator evaluate both readings.
2. **The open-space denominator is not resolved.** `open_space_basis` keeps three readings:
   the gross area before surrender, the gross area after surrender, the net area after every
   ownership deduction. ResolvedRules gives the area asked under each
   (`open_space.requirement_sqm_by_reading`). A conservative test profile may select the
   largest; the contract keeps the uncertainty.
3. **The PartitionLedger counts every square metre once, by its primary physical use.** A
   pavement that is both an internal road and the fire tender's route is one ROAD entry tagged
   FIRE_ACCESS. Buffer land or green-strip land that qualifies as open space is counted once,
   physically; its qualification is a QUALIFYING_OPEN_SPACE rule layer. Entries never overlap
   and sum to the net site area.
4. **Ownership reconciles by ownership only.** Gross ownership less SURRENDER, ACQUISITION and
   TRANSFER deductions (mutually exclusive, each with its location) is net ownership, the net
   site area. Setbacks, buffers, fire bands, green strips and other development restrictions
   never reduce it because building is barred there; they are rule layers.

### Contracts 1.1 (2026-10-03)

Found by the four streams working against 1.0, and approved with three clarifications. The
meaning lives in the contract as methods, so the optimizer and the validator cannot read it
differently.

1. **A height limit says three separate things** (`HeightLimit`): whether there is a number
   (`bound`: BOUNDED, UNBOUNDED, NOT_EVALUATED), whether the limit is in force (`applicability`:
   APPLIES, DOES_NOT_APPLY, or UNKNOWN when its condition rests on a site fact nobody settled),
   and how far the inputs behind the number are confirmed (`status`). One table judges a height
   for everyone (`HeightLimit.evaluate`): not applied when it does not apply; PASS within a
   bound, even when it is unknown whether the limit applies; FAIL beyond a bound that applies on
   confirmed inputs; UNVERIFIED beyond a bound that may apply, on unconfirmed inputs, or when
   the limit cannot be worked out. A generator does not offer a height beyond a bound that
   applies, confirmed or not (`HeightLimit.beyond`); beyond a bound that only may apply the
   height may be offered, labelled UNVERIFIED.
2. **A prohibited high-rise is not a permission for anything lower** (`HeightRules.high_rise`).
   Eligibility is ALLOWED, PROHIBITED or UNVERIFIED and follows from its grounds (the road, the
   plot size). PROHIBITED says only that no building of the high-rise height or more may stand
   here. What may be built below it (the permissible height, Table III setbacks, road
   conditions, spacing) is A2's to resolve and D2's to validate, each band with its own
   permission (`HeightRules.band_permission`); a band the rules do not model (18-21 m on most
   plots gives no setback) is never passed. The optimizer offers a count below 21 m on its own
   band's permission (C3), never on the site's high-rise eligibility.
3. **Every height falls in exactly one band** (`HeightRules.band_for`). Band edges say whether
   they are included: a building of exactly 21 m is a high-rise (rule 2(f)) with a band of its
   own, on Table IV's first row. An envelope band is matched to its rules band by its edges
   (`BuildableEnvelope.of_band`).
4. **A facility states its use and its surface; whether its ground counts as organised open
   space is derived** (`OpenSpaceRules.qualifies`), never declared and never read off a name.
   The rule names greenery, tot lot and soft landscaping, "etc.": those uses on soft ground
   count; whether a tot-lot must be soft (`tot_lot_surface`) and what the "etc." takes in
   (`open_space_other_uses`) are open readings evaluated every way, so the engine is never
   stricter than the text; a use or surface the firm's library does not state is UNKNOWN and
   the result UNVERIFIED. A facility's ledger ground follows its stated surface.
5. **Design margins are never law** (`DesignBrief.design_margins`, set in the firm's workspace
   file; none are invented). The optimizer aims at the legal minimum plus the margin; the
   validator judges against the legal minimum alone; a report shows the legal minimum, the
   target and what is provided (`ValidationReport.design_targets`). Each margin is the firm's
   standard or the engine's design assumption. The prototype generator applies none.
6. **Carried as read:** the clearance from electricity lines (rule 3(c)(i): 3 m high-tension,
   1.5 m low-tension, vertical and horizontal) and what a ramp in a side or rear setback leaves
   for fire vehicles (rule 13(c)(vii): 7 m). **Removed:** the 5% of site for amenities above
   5 acres, which is rule 9(o) and 10(i) (row and cluster housing) and does not apply here.
7. **Values that cannot be are refused:** coordinates that are not finite, a hole of two
   points, a bay under a metre, a parking measurement called law, shares outside their range,
   a cellar table with no row for the largest sites, objective priorities outside the named set.

The adapters follow: an access road's legal width is never marked confirmed merely because the
project gave no status (its status follows `abutting_road_status`, and is unverified when
nothing says); the brief and the placed facilities carry the use and surface the firm's
amenity library states, and nothing its names suggest.

### Contracts 1.2 (2026-10-04)

Made so Table III (rule 5) can be held, on A2's reading of the order and D2's needs. All
additions; a 1.1 document is refused like any older one.

1. **A band is read on its own measure.** Table IV's bands on the rule height; Table III's on
   the height above the stilt (`HeightMeasure.HEIGHT_ABOVE_STILT`), because rule 5(c) leaves the
   parking stilt out of Table III's heights whatever the reading of `stilt_in_rule_height`.
   `HeightRules.band_for_block` finds a block's band: its class from its rule height under the
   reading, then, below 21 m, its row on its height above the stilt.
2. **The front is held apart.** `Band.front_setback_m` is the setback on the access road's
   frontage (Table III's Building Line by road width; for a high-rise, rule 7(a)(xi)'s higher of
   Table IV and that line); `setback_m` is then every other side.
3. **A band says whether its heights may stand here** (`permission`, with a note when not
   ALLOWED); a height the tables do not permit is a band of its own, so the bands still cover
   every height once. `HeightRules.band_permission` adds the site's high-rise eligibility to a
   high-rise band; `permissible_non_high_rise` gives the highest band below 21 m that may stand.
4. **Table III's planting** (rule 5(f): 1 m along the frontage, 1 m round the rest above 300 m²)
   is carried in `GreenStripRules`, and what each band asks in `Band.green_strip_m`.
5. **Rule 8(l)'s pathways:** `RoadKind.PATHWAY` and `CirculationRules.pathway_width_m`.

The new legal values are optional fields the resolver fills; the contract holds none of them.

### Shared types and adapters

`common.py`: `Shape` (coordinates in the survey's metres), `Sourced`, `SourceKind` (SURVEY,
ARCHITECT, DOCUMENT, FIRM_STANDARD, FIRM_FINISHED_PLAN, ENGINE_DEFAULT, TEST_PROFILE), `Surface`
and `FacilityUse` (what a facility's ground is made of and what it is for), reuse of
`Provenance`, `Finding`, `Status` and `Basis` (moved to `basis.py` so the contracts import no
generator code). `src/siteplan/adapters/` converts Project, LayoutRequest and LayoutOption to
the contracts and back; `option_keys` keeps the chat tool's reply. Contract fixtures on made-up
land (`tests/contract_fixtures/`: a rectangle with a surrendered strip, an L-plot with a 24 m
arm, a plot crossed by a nala, a plot under 4,000 m²) hold an instance of every contract.

## 5. Dependencies and merge order

```
P0  integration (done): contracts, fixtures, adapters, baseline, test classes, leak guard
 │
 ├── A1 Legal envelope ───────────────────────────────┐
 │     └── A2 Table III (non-high-rise rules) ──┐     │
 ├── B  Prototype library ──────────────────────┼─────┤
 ├── D  Validator ─── gates every C merge       │     │
 │     └── D2 non-high-rise checks (needs A2) ──┤     │
 └── C1 Optimizer core (on adapters and contract fixtures)
        └── C2 real envelope, prototypes, joint circulation (needs A1, B; D gates)
               └── C3 blocks below 21 m (needs A2, D2): the mixed-height optimizer complete
```

A1, B, D and C1 start in parallel after P0, against the contracts and the contract fixtures.
Merge order: P0, D, A1, B, C1, C2, A2, D2, C3. A contract changes only through integration,
with a version bump; every branch then takes the change in (a merge of the integration branch,
since pushed branches are never rewritten) and is retested alone before it is integrated.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

**A1 Legal envelope.** (1) Imports nothing from towers, layout, grounds, the optimizer or
access's road generators (an AST test). (2) No floor count in ResolvedRules. (3) Every rule
value has clause, basis and status; every open reading an Interpretation; the three required
ALL readings are ALL. (4) Dhulapally, 60 ft USER_CONFIRMED: the rule-height limit is 30 m on the
18.29 m road (Table IV as substituted by G.O.50/2019; the next band needs 24 m); high-rise from
21 m; a group development scheme; the 30 m physical limit applies only if the road ends at the
plot (UNVERIFIED), as do the street join and the airport height; no floor count asserted in A1.
(5) Suchitra: the nala buffer is an exclusion; its 12.38 m road's limit (24 m) carries the
road's UNVERIFIED status. (6) A taller band's buildable land lies inside a shorter band's;
non-high-rise bands present as not modelled until A2. (7) Dhulapally's northern arm appears in
the width profile with its area and widths per band, in no exclusion or reservation; no
"unusable" class exists. (8) Circulation as requirements only. (9) Rule layers with
permissions, setback roads CONDITIONAL; open-space requirement per reading. (10) A debug drawing
(DXF and SVG): original site, net site, exclusions, setback envelope and buildable land per
band, access zones, rule layers, the open-space quantity per reading.

**A2 Table III** (planned dependency; scoped reading, no broad research). Table III (rule 5)
and the non-high-rise provisions the optimizer needs (setbacks by plot size and height, gaps
between non-high-rise blocks in a GDS, fire access below 21 m through rule 15(a)(i), rule 8(l)
below 12 m), each checked against the orders held (G.O.7/2016, 50/2019, 65/2019, 95/2026), the
unread ones recorded as residual risk; values in rules.py with clause and page, pinned in
test_rules.py; the gap between a low block and a high-rise an interpretation; a made-up test
where only a Table III block fits a narrow region.

**B Prototypes.** Round trips; footprint = modules + cores + corridor within 2%; per-floor
numbers from the modules; at least single-core small (about 4 flats a floor), single-core
(about 6), two-core medium (about 8), two-core large (10 or more), built from the made-up
example library in tests and the firm's library at run time; nothing derived from a Dhulapally
file or `flat_library.calibrated.json`; no legal value or floor count; valid when rotated and
mirrored; a DXF block each; LEGACY_RECTANGLE reproduces today's towers.

**C1 Optimizer core.** Floors from metres: each tower gets the whole feasible set (made-up
3.0 m stilt and floors: a 30 m rule limit gives 9 floors above the stilt when it counts and 10
when not; the latter's 33 m physical height is UNVERIFIED with the dead end unknown, FAIL with a
dead end); a planted higher-scoring candidate with a FAIL is never returned; three Pareto
alternatives that `same_idea` calls different; the legacy strategy reproduces the pinned debug
baseline; deterministic per seed, within a time budget.

**C2 Full search.** Tower count, prototype, position, rotation and floors per tower, mixed
heights (a made-up site where one part suits only a lower band; lowering one tower to fit
another is found when it pays; the legal maximum never means every tower at it); circulation
generated with the towers and open space, never boundary → setback → fixed loop → towers; open
space, club house, ramp and amenities placed by search within the rule-layer permissions, under
both readings of `circulation_in_setback`; nothing D fails is offered; every option states its
UNVERIFIED items and the readings they rest on; alternatives that hold under every reading, and
one per reading where the reading changes the answer.

**C3 Below 21 m** (needs A2, D2). Low blocks wherever the envelope allows; on the made-up
L-plot the 24 m arm is evaluated for a low block and the objective decides.

**D Validator.** Inputs: site model, rules, brief, candidate; an envelope only to cross-check.
Imports no generator module (an AST test); the measuring geometry it borrows from access.py is
rebuilt inside `validator/`. Recomputes from the site model: the net plot, water buffers, each
tower's heights under every open reading, setbacks, gaps (taller governs), road widths, fire
bands and turns, qualifying open space under each denominator, built-up, Table V and the cars
laid out, units by type from the embedded prototypes, the club house rule, the PartitionLedger
and RuleLayers. An envelope with a deliberately wrong setback does not make it pass a tower that
breaches the true one. Planted violations each FAIL (setback 0.3 m short, short gap, an 8.9 m
road, a bay in a fire band, a tower above its band under both readings, open space at 9.9%,
short parking, a ramp in the front setback). Reading-dependent results are UNVERIFIED with the
reading named. A missed mix changes the program verdict only. On the characterization baseline
it gives today's checker's statuses or documents each difference; on `fixtures/cases` the same
known-FAIL list. **D2** adds the non-high-rise checks after A2.

## 7. What in the prototype works against this design

1. `LayoutRequest` mixes site facts, legal readings and design intent: it stays the legacy
   internal type, built by `adapters.request` from the three contracts.
2. The rule code thinks in floors (`max_floors.FloorLimit`, `heights.legal_findings`,
   `search_heights`): legal outputs move to metres; FloorLimit stays a labelled presentation.
3. The generator certifies itself (`layout._check` builds the checker's input from its option).
4. The checker shares the generator's geometry (checks → access_checks → access).
5. Narrow land is deleted before any building is tried (`grounds.py`, the 9 m opening of the
   towers' land): legacy strategy only.
6. The loop road is fixed before the towers (`grounds.frame`, `access.loop_road`,
   `access.entrance`): legacy strategy only.
7. One height for every tower (`LayoutRequest.floors`, `SiteFacts`, the inset in
   `grounds.frame`): needs per-band envelopes and per-tower floors.
8. Table III was unread: A2 read it, and C3 places blocks below 21 m.
9. Dhulapally's blind acceptance waits on the strip's location, which only the architect has;
   the debug baseline carries the regression meanwhile.
10. Shapely objects inside dataclasses: the contracts carry coordinates; the adapters convert.
11. Per-tower floors multiply the search: a two-level search and budgets.
12. Hermes needs `OPTION_KEYS`: `adapters.option_keys` keeps them.
13. The client fixtures exist only on the firm's machine: streams run there as local agents.
14. A profile's single "layout" section mixes brief values and legal readings: it splits into
    site, brief and interpretations when the profiles move onto the contracts.

## 8. Rules for every stream

- Its own worktree and branch, draft PRs only; never push to main or force-push; the owner
  merges.
- Client data stays in `fixtures/` and `out/`; tests use made-up geometry and skip cleanly
  without client files.
- No existing rules.py value changes; new constants only with their primary source and test.
- A characterization test changes only with a normative replacement and an entry in
  `docs/behaviour-changes.md`.
- No `Co-Authored-By` or `Claude-Session` lines in commits or PRs.
